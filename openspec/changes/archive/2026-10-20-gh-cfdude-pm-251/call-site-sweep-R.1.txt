## Where the basis applies and where total stays on purpose
- APPLIES: tokenSignal() (spend-guard.mjs) counts status.work or status.total by the resolved tokenBasis and takes its baseline from tokenBaseline(epic, basis). It is the ONLY reader of the ceiling besides resolveGuard()/guardConfigured() (which treat tokenCeiling as a limit and tokenBasis as no limit) and the verbs.
- STAYS total ON PURPOSE: spend-report.mjs coverage.attributed/total and the byX dimension sort (conductor-13 forbids a second sum of .total; they describe the ledger, not a ceiling); rowTotal() itself; the `spend` text header line.
- ONE definition of work: rowWork() in spend-reader.mjs, summed once in addTo(); no consumer re-sums it.

## Data references: tokenBasis on state.spendGuard / state.laneSpendGuards[lane] / epic.spendGuard, and basis on a tokens override entry
- WRITERS of tokenBasis: applyGuardOps() (set-spend-guard) only. READERS: resolveGuard() (via CHECKS.tokenBasis), lowCeilingNotice(), the rules/brief emit nothing from it (a basis alone is not a limit, guardConfigured ignores it).
- REMOVE paths of tokenBasis: set-spend-guard --unset token-basis (per layer) and --unset all (GUARD_FIELDS now includes it); an emptied layer is deleted; remove-epic takes epic.spendGuard along.
- WRITER of override.basis: overrideSpendGuard() (tokens only), from sig.basis. READER: tokenBaseline(). REMOVE: an override is never deleted; --revoke marks revokedAt and keeps it (the entry and its basis stay).

## Inverses
- --token-basis has --unset token-basis (and --unset all). An override has --revoke (existing). The low-ceiling notice is output, not state: no inverse.

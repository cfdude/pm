INVERSES — task 4.2. Every operation this change ADDS or MODIFIES, with its inverse named and
its shipping status justified. An inverse not shipped and not justified here is a FINDING; this
change's whole thesis is that the call-site sweep structurally cannot reach this class, because
enumerating the callers of a thing that is WRITTEN never leads to asking whether it can be UNwritten.

════ VERB / FLAG PAIRS ════

  --preauthorize  ↔  --revoke                         SHIPS. Pair complete (1.2).
  --revoke        ↔  re-granting                      SHIPS, and it is the DOCUMENTED un-revoke:
      granting again writes a NEW grant and leaves the revocation readable beside it. This is why
      a revoke marks only the UNREVOKED matches — a live and a revoked entry for one action
      legitimately coexist, and re-stamping the set would overwrite an earlier reason and date.
  push-detour     ↔  drop-detour                      SHIPS. Pair complete (3.4).
  drop-detour     ↔  re-pushing the frame             NOT SHIPPED AS AN UNDO, and justified:
      `push-detour` already creates a frame, so the capability exists; what is deliberately absent
      is an "un-drop" that would RESURRECT an obligation somebody deliberately ended with a
      required reason. Restoring it would have to invent who ended it and why it is owed again.
      (`push-detour` refuses an epic already on the stack; after a drop it does not, so re-pausing
      the epic is reachable. What is not reachable is reviving the ANSWERED-NOTHING obligation,
      which is the part that must not be.)
  a grants clear-all                                  NOT SHIPPED. Deletion under another name;
      design Decision 1 rules it out. Splicing would make "authorised and then taken back"
      indistinguishable from "never authorised", which is the evidence a safety record exists for.
  --context, --notify                                 NO INVERSE, and the `epic-autonomy` spec
      scopes its inverse claim to `--preauthorize` for exactly this reason rather than writing a
      blanket sentence that would leave both silently uncovered. Both are declared `repeats: true`
      with NO `key` (constants.mjs:741-742, re-checked at sweep time), so `update-epic --clear`
      cannot reach either and nothing un-writes them. JUSTIFICATION: each is an APPEND-ONLY RECORD
      OF SOMETHING THAT HAPPENED — a note, a notification — not a standing authorisation. An
      unrevoked grant authorises an action tomorrow; a note about yesterday does not. There is
      nothing live to take back. That difference in KIND is the whole reason a grant needs a revoke.
  --level autonomous ↔ --level off                    SHIPS; the flag is its own inverse. `off`
      deliberately does NOT clear grants (Decision 2) — that would be deletion, and the measured
      harm was the SILENT restore, which is fixed at the re-arm instead.

════ FIELD WRITES — pushDetour() performs FIVE, and the sweep above cannot reach them ════
An inverse is owed PER WRITE, not per verb.

  1. the detour-stack frame          → drop-detour REMOVES it. (Also pop-detour, for the other case.)
  2. paused.reconcileNeeded          → drop-detour ENDS it, by recompute, in the same write.
  3. paused.status = "paused"        → NOT UN-DONE BY drop-detour, DELIBERATELY (scenario 3.3
       "Dropping a frame does not resume or revive the epic"). Justified: the drop's whole case is
       that the epic is NOT coming back, so restoring a live status would be the record claiming
       work restarted. The inverse of the status write EXISTS and is `pop-detour` (resume) or
       `update-epic --status <s>` (say what it became) — two verbs, each for a different truth,
       and neither is this one's job. An operation that both ended the pause and set a status
       would be making a judgment the caller has not made.
  4. detour.role = "detour"          → NOT UN-DONE, and "obviously fine" is not the answer this
       change accepts. The reasoning: `role` is HISTORICAL, not control state. It records that this
       epic WAS built as a detour, which stays true after the frame ends — the work happened. It is
       read by links.mjs detourContext() as a FALLBACK when no frame is live (so clearing it would
       change a rendered answer about the past) and by render.mjs's role column. Un-setting it would
       assert the epic was never a detour, which is false. There is also no verb that sets it —
       `add-epic` has no `--role` — so an inverse would be the only half of an unpaired operation,
       pointing the other way. NOT A FINDING: the write is a record, not an authorisation.
  5. TWO links via linkOnce(): paused `may-invalidate` detour, detour `resolves-blocker-for` paused
       5a. paused's `may-invalidate`      → DISARMED by drop-detour and KEPT, carrying the drop
             stamp. Removing it is REFUSED by gate-integrity's "A write never destroys the record
             of an owed reconcile", so disarm-and-keep is the only inverse available and it is the
             right one: the evidence an obligation existed must outlive the obligation.
       5b. detour's `resolves-blocker-for` → NOT UN-DONE. Justified on the same ground as `role`
             and stated rather than assumed: it is an ANNOTATION band link (links.mjs
             LINK_TYPES_ANNOTATION) — nothing switches on it — and it records that this detour was
             spawned to unblock that epic, which remains true however the pause ended. It is also
             not orphaned by the drop: the paused epic still exists and still holds its own side.
             An inverse EXISTS for a caller who wants one (`update-epic <detour> --clear-links`,
             all-or-nothing) and is deliberately not invoked here, because the drop must not
             silently delete an edge the caller did not name.

════ THIS CHANGE'S OWN NEW WRITES ════
  the `revoked` stamp on a grant     → no un-revoke that ERASES it, by design (see --revoke above).
       Re-granting is the forward-only inverse. Erasing would destroy the safety record.
  the `dropped` stamp on a link      → same shape, same reason: it is the record that the
       obligation was ended rather than answered, and `record-reconcile` still refuses the link
       (through its existing unarmed arm), so nothing can overwrite it with a verdict.
  the write-time reference refusals  → guards, not writes. A guard's inverse is not shipping it.
  the three integrity checks         → read-only, report-never-repair. Nothing to invert.

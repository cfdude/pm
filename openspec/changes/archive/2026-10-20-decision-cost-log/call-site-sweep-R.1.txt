## Conclusions
- WRITERS of `epic.decisions`: `record-decision` only (append in recordDecision; the withdrawal mark in withdraw()). No other
  verb writes it; `update-epic` cannot (the `decisions` registry row is engineWritten and set-only, so `--decisions` is an
  unknown flag and `--clear decisions` is refused by name).
- READERS: `decisions` (decisionsVerb) and `spend-analysis --epic` (analyze -> costDecisions), both through
  `costDecisions`/`renderDecisionLines`, so the two outputs cannot differ. Neither stores a cost.
- REMOVE path: `remove-epic` deletes the epic record and with it its decisions; nothing else holds a decision number (no other
  record stores `n`, a decision id or a pointer to a decision), so no dangling reference is possible. The cost lives only in
  the ledger and is recomputed on each read; `spend --clear` therefore removes costs but keeps the decisions (asserted in
  scripts/test/assert/decision-log.test.mjs).
- Ingest callers: `decisions` and `spend-analysis` both reach the ledger through `ingestForReport`, so developer-log capture is
  decided in one place; with tracking off neither is called (asserted by observe-reads proofs on both rungs).
- INVERSES. append <-> withdraw (shipped; withdraw marks and keeps, as an override revoke does). Not shipped, justified:
  (a) an EDIT verb: a decision is a record of what was decided, a correction is a withdrawal plus a new entry (design D4);
  (b) an UNWITHDRAW: a withdrawal is itself a record of a judgement, and re-entering the decision as a new numbered entry keeps
  both visible; (c) no `--clear`: set-only by the registry row, with its reason.
